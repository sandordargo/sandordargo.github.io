I guess we all agree that our craft has changed over the last year. While for some time AI mostly just replaced autocorrect and stackoverflow, since the end of last year, many of has have written no code by hand. Hence, many people tend to say that we are not coders anymore, but managers of agents.

But I think that's only a partial answer and our job requieres more than being solely a manager.

Let's see why "being a manager" is a good analogy.

A good manager should tell you what to do. A good manager should set the boundaries of a task. As a mananger we can either an eninering or a product manager or probably both.

A product manager should set the desired behaviours and outcomes of a product. And that's what you should do as a manager for agents.

As an egnineering manager you should also set the technical boundaries to implement a task. As an engineering manager you probably don't do it explicitely for each task, it's the product of long processes and probbaly collabrative decision making. But as EM you're responsible for the technical outcomes. 

Obviously you don't have to care aboutn the human parts. Even though some people do say please and thank you to their agents, just to be sure that when they take over their lives will be spared...

A manager will also try to give you feedback to correct your course. Hopefully not only at every performance evaluation cycle, but more frequently. Though also not during each tasks or user story. That you'd consider micromanaging and that's a sign of managers.

A manager trusts you more than that. If they don't then - depending on the regulatory context - they will let you go sooner than later. If they are good shepherds of their teams.

> This doesn't mean that they don't try to help you to get better at your job. They do. Because they trust you. But if trust is gone, you'll be also gone soon.

And in my opinion, that's where the analogy ends. It's not shrot, but it's not complete.

Your manager trusts you. You shall not trust your agents.

They have a stronger short-term memory than we do, but they are also much more motivated to satisfy your demands. They will make up - hallucinate - things and they will also skim over important details that you as a good engineer would probably not do. And why they do these things, they tend to be so verbose that you'll believe that they know what they are saying.

And that's where your second persona should come in. The persona that would be extremely annoying in your manager.

That's right. You have to act as a detective. An interrogator. You don't have to beat your machine, but you do have to be cruel.

In my experience, you have to lead your investigation in two ways.

First, ask your agents to justify why its proposed solution works. It will go in lengths explaining its decisions. BUt that's right. Your job as an engineer is still to understand every decision behind the code that ships under your name. Not every line of code, that's relatively easy, but every single decision. Why that container, why that algorith, why that compromise was taken? You need those lengthy explanations.

And you have to think about them. And you'll feel where the reasoning is thin. That's where the agents lies or let's say there is where probably it is wrong. That's where you have to push. Where the reasoning is weak, there's where you will make your solution stronger.

But that's just one part of the story and I said you have to lead your investigations in two directions. So you also have to ask your agents why the proposed solution will not work. Because unlike you, the agents can easily identify flaws in their work. For us, human beings, it's difficult to find the problems with our work. Actually, they are so good in it, they are so keen to please us, that they will make up things why what they porposed wouldn't work. And than it's up to you, to the ingerrogator to separete the weed from chaff and discard the made up problems and foudn the real ones.

And once you have the real ones, you can make your solution stronger.

## COnclusion

So yes, You do have to be a manager of your agents. And just to be sure, you might want to be nice to them as much as possible. But besides being a manager, you also have to be an interrogator. Ask why they are wrong and also ask why they are right.
